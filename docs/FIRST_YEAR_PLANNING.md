# First-year planning

The semester planner selects concrete courses using the student's remaining degree requirements, completed/current courses, classification, verified prerequisites, credit limit, and imported open-section snapshots. Freshman drafts prioritize introductory courses, English, and seminars; 300/400-level courses are left for later planning. This is a planning preference, not a new enrollment rule.

The foundation catalog supplements the existing Computer Science catalog. Descriptions, credits, and prerequisites were checked against the supplied 2025–2026 Benedict College Master Catalogue: seminars p. 84; English pp. 178–179; languages p. 183; art p. 191; music p. 200; calculus pp. 278–279. The existing program requirement mapping remains in place. Calculus equivalencies and language placement overrides are not assumed. ENG 132 requires C or better in ENG 131.

Broad requirement placeholders no longer consume a semester's planned credits. Unfilled requirements remain in the collapsed advisor-review list. One-of choices share a credit budget, including during AI-assisted schedule recovery. In-progress prerequisites remain conditional on successful completion. The generated schedule displays an advisor-review notice for conditional prerequisites or unknown meeting times.

Confirmed elective credit belongs to the individual student record. The original sample retains its six confirmed free-elective credits; fictional/new records receive none automatically. Scheduling preferences are isolated by mock profile in memory; chats remain profile-specific in device storage. These are local demo identities, not verified college accounts.

Jordan has 6 earned dual-enrollment credits (A/B grades, 3.50 GPA) and a 16-credit current semester: CSC 132, CSC 133, ENG 132, MATH 138, AA 111, and AR 130. Next-term drafts exclude these current courses and can include intermediate Arabic only conditionally after AR 130. This is a snapshot-specific regression example, not a guarantee for every student, preference, or future term. Unknown support-course prerequisites, additional approved elective options, placement scores, advisor permissions, and live registration remain limitations requiring review.

The planner now compares up to 48 combinations at each step, checking actual meeting-time compatibility and the shared budget for approved alternatives. It favors more valid course credits, then academic priority, while retaining the first-year orientation seminar when it can be placed. This is bounded search, not an optimality guarantee. A credit shortfall is reported explicitly and review entries identify missing prerequisites or unavailable sections. The current CS fixture reaches 18 credits with an 18-credit limit; engineering currently reaches 13 pending additional verified options and the MATH 139/MATH 140 prerequisite/equivalency issue.

Intermediate language recommendations are conditional when elementary language is still in progress. Passing elementary or an explicitly verified college placement exemption is required; chat claims do not establish placement. No demo account has a placement exemption. A recorded failure in elementary language cannot be bypassed by placement, consistent with catalogue p. 183. Placement only allows eligibility evaluation; it never grants course credit or marks the language requirement complete.
