# Computer Engineering demo

Select **Use engineering test student** or use `demo.engineering@my.benedict.edu` in the mock login. Alex Morgan is a fictional freshman with 9 earned dual-enrollment credits and 16 in progress. Identity, advisor history and in-memory schedule preferences are separate from the existing test students. This remains an unverified local demo login.

The selected program now follows the student's major across Home, recommendations, degree progress, planning, schedule recovery and advisor context. Computer Science keeps its existing mapping. Computer Engineering uses the supplied Benedict College 2025–26 Master Catalogue program table (p. 292): 125 total credits, 77 major credits, including a 3-credit approved major elective. The current semester includes CSC 132, MATH 139, ENGR 130, ENG 132, AA 111, and AR 130. GPA is 3.33 from the three completed 3-credit courses (B, A, B). All required courses and the three approved major-elective options now have course rules in the engineering catalog. Engineering rules are from pp. 287–291, physics from p. 273, support mathematics from pp. 279–280 and history from p. 227. Shared CS/foundation rules are reused only where the course appears in this degree. The Calculus I requirement preserves the program table’s C minimum.

The program’s required courses, credits, prerequisites, class-standing restrictions and lab corequisites are mapped. Labs bring a valid required lecture into the same planning bundle unless the lecture has been passed or is conditional on the current term. The final timetable rejects orphan labs, full sections and time conflicts. Graduating-senior design clearance and instructor permission still require verification. No Computer Science-only course is inserted to fill the engineering schedule. Availability is an imported snapshot, not live registration data. Catalog, placement and permissions must be reviewed before enrollment.


With the current record and imported Spring sections, the planner can place 16 credits at a 16/18-credit target, including MATH 236 (its catalog prerequisite is MATH 138, which Alex completed). At a 15-credit limit it places up to 15. The catalog itself lists Precalculus as MATH 139 but Calculus I references MATH 140 or equivalent; this discrepancy remains explicit rather than silently waived.

The in-tab Gemini helper accepts a request such as “Get me to 16 credits.” The client preserves one unambiguous numeric target in the supported 12–18 range, then reruns the deterministic planner against sections, lab companions, degree budgets, eligibility and meeting times. Gemini’s course/section suggestions guide preferences without overriding those checks. A shortfall remains visible, and moved requirements remain flagged for review.
